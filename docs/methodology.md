# Methodology

Every ready model in `config/models/` (currently eight, including GEOSCCM and EMAC) is an independent CCMI-2022 refD1 primary model source, and WACCM-X is processed the same way as a separate whole-atmosphere source. The `harmonize` step remaps **each model separately** onto a shared product format — 5° area-weighted latitude bands and a 1 km altitude grid — over the model's own validated vertical range. There is no average between models, no vertical blend with WACCM-X and no extension above a model's own top. Native model products remain unchanged. Satellite climatologies are evaluation data only. They never fill cells or change production climatologies. Direct satellite fusion is inappropriate here because observing periods, instrument biases, retrieval characteristics, trends in long-lived gases, and vertical/temporal overlap differ.

```text
CCMI refD1 ──→ monthly zonal series ──→ native climatology ──→ harmonize ──→ harmonized NetCDF
WACCM-X ────→ monthly zonal series ──→ native climatology ──→ harmonize ──→ harmonized NetCDF
```


## Shared format, not a shared ceiling

The harmonized product replaces the former pressure-grid application product. Models share the horizontal and vertical **format** — 36 area-weighted 5° latitude bands and 1 km altitude spacing — while each keeps its own **vertical range**: 0 km up to the highest level the model's climatology resolves. Nothing is padded to a common top and nothing is clipped at one. The project-wide log-pressure axis in `config/climatology.yaml` survives only as a guard: `cf.assert_product` still refuses a native climatology whose level axis already sits on it, so an individual product can never be mistaken for a remapped one, and the monthly zonal intermediates stay on native levels throughout.
## Native statistics stay on native levels

**Native statistics are not interpolated during `build`.** The monthly zonal series keeps the vertical grid of its model and the calendar-month `mean`, population `sigma`, `minimum` and `maximum` are accumulated on those native levels, so the vertical step is left out of the pipeline entirely rather than performed twice: the global attributes state it (`vertical_interpolation` begins `none; every statistic is computed on the native vertical levels of the model`, `vertical_extrapolation` begins `none; a level the model does not reach simply does not exist here`), and a level a model does not reach simply has no row in that file. The harmonize step later maps those native statistics onto the harmonized altitude grid. `n_years` is a true coverage count on the native level, and `finite_fraction` in the build report quantifies what is missing rather than filling it. Each `<variable>_mean` field records the vertical treatment of its own source: `on_application_pressure_grid: "false"`, `vertical_treatment: climatological statistics on the native vertical levels of the model`, `native_level_dimension`, `native_level_count`, `native_vertical_coordinate`, `native_pressure_units` and `native_pressure_kind` — `coordinate` when the level coordinate itself carries pressure in Pa, `profile` when a `(time, level, lat)` `air_pressure` field does, as on hybrid grids — plus the observed `native_pressure_min_pa`/`native_pressure_max_pa`. A model that keeps two unrelated vertical grids under the *same* source level name — GEOSCCM carries its chemistry on 39 pressure levels and its dynamics on 72 hybrid levels and calls both dimensions `lev` — is published with one dimension per grid instead of a merged union of 111 levels that would turn every field of the other grid into rows of NaN: the first grid keeps the source name and the others are numbered `lev_2`, `lev_3`, ..., with nothing resampled and the source name still in `native_vertical_coordinate`. A grid is recognised by the pressures of its levels, never by its name and never by its level count alone: two grids of the same length whose pressures differ are two grids, a grid whose pressures agree within a relative tolerance is one grid whatever its source name, and the *representation* of the pressure belongs to the identity of a grid too: a grid whose levels are a fixed pressure and a grid whose levels move are two grids even when their pressures agree level by level, because a CF dimension coordinate applies to every variable that shares its dimension, so publishing a pressure-valued `lev` coordinate over a variable whose pressure is a `(time, level, lat)` profile would state a pressure for it that is not its pressure. The coordinate grid keeps the source name and the profile grid takes the next number, so the split is decided by what the pressure is, not by which variable was read first. `vertical_level_counts` lists every grid of the product (`lev=39; lev_2=72`) while `vertical_level_count` remains the deepest one, and the coordinate of a grid whose pressure lives only in a field is the plain level index of the model (`units: 1`, `positive: down`) with that field published alongside it as `air_pressure_<dimension>` and named in the field's `pressure_field`; a variable whose levels come from the coordinate instead names it in `pressure_coordinate`, and a field never names both. An index coordinate carries `units: 1`, no `standard_name`, and a `comment` that points at the field holding the pressure of its levels, and when two grids share one source name the coordinate of each says in its own `comment` that the variables of the other take their pressure from their own dimension — the one place a reader cannot miss it, because a shared name is otherwise easy to read across. Every part is renamed and pinned to the canonical coordinate of its grid **before** the fields are merged, so an equal level name hiding two representations can never be unioned into one axis of mixed rows, and a part whose level count disagrees with the grid it was registered on is refused by variable name. Each `<variable>_mean` also carries `native_source_level_dimension`, the name that grid had in the model, which for the second and later grids differs from the published `native_level_dimension`. Two-dimensional fields (tropopause pressure and height, and similar scalar-per-column fields) keep their own `time, lat` shape, get no vertical dimension, and are labelled `vertical_treatment: two-dimensional field kept without a vertical dimension`. Where native levels are mapped to another vertical coordinate — the harmonize step — the vertical rules are shared and strict: interpolation is linear per latitude column and per climatological month, **never extrapolates**, and treats a non-finite or non-positive pressure as a barrier that is dropped rather than bridged, so an internal gap in a model's published mask stays a gap and a column with fewer than two valid points is NaN.

## One file per model and period

Each `data/processed/MODEL/.../VARIABLE_monthly_zonal.nc` retains time, latitude, native level, native units, source file names, and model identity, and the climatology product is written on those same native levels — nothing is regridded anywhere in the build. `jumacs climatology --model GEOSCCM` (and the climatology step of `jumacs build`) writes **exactly one file per model and period**: `products/climatology/GEOSCCM/jumacs_geosccm_refd1_climatology_1985-2014.nc`. There are no per-variable files any more, and no groups — one ordinary NetCDF dataset holds every processed variable as `<variable>_mean`, `<variable>_sigma`, `<variable>_minimum`, `<variable>_maximum`, `<variable>_n_years`, so a single file can be opened once and indexed by species.

## Climatological time axis and CF conventions

The file follows **CF-1.13** (`Conventions` attribute) and, most importantly, uses a **climatological time axis** instead of a fake twelve-month time series. `time` is a float64 coordinate variable with `units = "days since 2000-01-01"` (the configured nominal reference year), `calendar = "gregorian"`, `axis = "T"`, `standard_name = "time"` and `climatology = "climatology_bounds"`; the twelve cells are the twelve calendar months, not twelve dates. `climatology_bounds(time, nv)` brackets each cell with the start and end of that month taken from a **common non-leap year**, so February always has 28 days and the annual cycle has exactly twelve intervals; the bounds variable carries the same `units`/`calendar` and deliberately no fill value. Which statistic each field holds is stated the CF way, in `cell_methods`, so no statistic name is invented inside `standard_name`: `<variable>_mean` is `longitude: mean time: mean within years time: mean over years`, `<variable>_sigma` uses `time: standard_deviation over years`, `<variable>_minimum`/`<variable>_maximum` use `time: minimum over years`/`time: maximum over years`, and `<variable>_n_years` is `longitude: mean time: sample_size over years` with `units = "1"` and no `standard_name` (it is a count). `long_name` spells the statistic out in words, e.g. `"Ozone mixing ratio: climatological standard deviation"`.

`standard_name` is published **only when the source name is genuinely CF**: `cf.valid_standard_name` accepts a curated list plus the regular `mole_fraction_of_*_in_air`/`mass_mixing_ratio_of_*_in_air` pattern, and drops anything else so the field keeps an accurate `long_name` rather than a fabricated standard name. No CF table is downloaded and no name is invented. Where an archive names its fields with nothing CF-like — WACCM-X keeps raw CAM-chem names such as `O3`, `T`, `PS`, `OMEGA` — the correspondence is declared explicitly in a `standard_names:` section of that model's configuration (`config/waccmx.yaml`), and `cf.curated_standard_name` publishes such a name **only when the field's `units` match what that CF name requires** (`mol mol-1` for a mole fraction, `K` for `air_temperature`, `m2 s-2` for `geopotential_height`, which is why WACCM-X `Z3`, stored in metres, stays unnamed). Each published name records its origin in `standard_name_source`, `archive` or `config`. All three-dimensional fields are ordered `(time, <native level>, lat)`, where `<native level>` is the level coordinate of the model itself — float64, `units = "Pa"`, `standard_name = "air_pressure"`, `positive = "down"`, `axis = "Z"` when the model publishes pressure on that coordinate, otherwise a plain level dimension whose pressure is published as a separate `(time, <native level>, lat)` `air_pressure` field in Pa — and `lat` (`degrees_north`, `axis = "Y"`, float64); two-dimensional fields are `(time, lat)`. Statistics have fixed storage: `float32` fields with a NaN fill value and compressed, `int16` for `n_years`, float64 coordinates. Model-wide provenance (source dataset/archive, experiment, institution, coverage, period, `reference_period_years`, `nominal_reference_year`, grid description and level count, the interpolation and extrapolation rules, missing-value policy, `variable_count`, processing history) is recorded **once as global attributes** rather than repeated per variable; `bias_correction`, `trend_correction` and `model_combination` are `none`. Global `time_coverage_start`/`time_coverage_end` delimit the **climatological period** (for the reference product `1985-01-01` and `2014-12-31`), because that is the interval the file describes; the usually longer span of the underlying monthly series is kept separately in `source_time_coverage_start`/`source_time_coverage_end` as evidence of the sampling behind the means. The writer refuses a product whose coverage attributes disagree with `climatology_period` and `reference_period_years`.

The writer is strict. It writes to a `.nc.tmp` sibling, **reopens the finished file without CF decoding** so the validators see exactly what is on disk, and refuses the product unless the conventions, the twelve-cell float64 time axis with its named bounds variable, a vertical axis that is genuinely the model's own (`air_pressure` in Pa with `positive = "down"` and `axis = "Z"`, finite, positive and free of repeated levels, or a named pressure field sharing the field's dimensions), the absence of the application grid, `lat` units, the presence of every processed variable, the statistic dtypes, `cell_methods`, units, dimension order and the absence of any per-variable `*_air_pressure` field all check out — only then is the temporary renamed over the destination, so a failed run never leaves a half-written or non-CF product. Because there is now one file per model, previously generated application, compact and per-variable products are obsolete and must be regenerated with `jumacs build` and `jumacs harmonize`.

## Harmonized altitude-grid products

`jumacs harmonize --model SOCOL --start-year 1985 --end-year 2014` (or `--model all`, which adds WACCM-X) reads one validated native climatology and writes one harmonized NetCDF to `products/harmonized/SOCOL/jumacs_socol_1985-2014_5deg_1km.nc`. The target grid is fixed: twelve climatological months, 36 area-weighted 5° latitude bands, and 1 km altitude levels from 0 km to the model's own valid top — the highest altitude at which the climatology still holds finite values, derived from the model's geopotential-height field and recorded as `model_valid_top_km`. Surface fields and `geopotential_height` itself stay in the native product; harmonized fields carry the four statistics `mean`, `sigma`, `minimum` and `maximum` as compressed float32.

Altitude comes from the model's own geopotential height, never from a scale-height guess. A field stored as geopotential (`m2 s-2`) is divided by standard gravity, and a U.S. Standard Atmosphere sanity check flags a field whose values sit a factor of about 9.8 off. Each field level's altitude is found by reading the model's height profile as a function of `-log(pressure)` per month and latitude band, and values are interpolated linearly between valid levels along each month/latitude column. Interpolation never extrapolates: below the lowest and above the highest valid level the grid stays NaN, and interior gaps are preserved rather than bridged. Global attributes state the treatment (`product_type=harmonized_climatology`, `vertical_extent_km`, `model_valid_top_km`, no bias or trend correction), and the writer validates the finished file against the harmonized contract before publishing it.

There is deliberately no vertical blend with WACCM-X and no completion above a model's top: the earlier application, compact and application-ready pipelines (including the IG2 upper-tail completion) are removed and their outputs are obsolete. A harmonized file ends where its model's evidence ends.
